# Style brief: the hand-drawn look (test round, 2026-09-25)

This brief overrides the playbook's "visual language" (its type roles,
palette, and backgrounds) for this video only. Every other rule in the
playbook still holds: one thing moves at a time, picture before name, cue
then change, holds sized to what moves, caption band below y 918, contrast,
no flashing, no emojis, cross-references describe the concept.

## What the user asked for

"Something a little more unique and hand drawn", like the minutephysics
video "The Physics of Dissonance" (around 2:00 onward). What that video
does, frame by frame:

- Everything is drawn in marker as the narrator speaks: words are hand
  lettered, pictures are simple line doodles (stick figures, a speaker, a
  bee, a dog, a board, thought and speech bubbles, arrows, braces,
  squiggles).
- Doodles are tiny, fast, and funny-by-simplicity: a stick figure with a
  thought bubble holding "A 220?" says more than a paragraph.
- Only one or two spot colors (there: orange and blue) mark the two things
  being compared; everything else is the plain marker color.
- Most of the frame is empty ground. One idea per frame; when the idea
  changes, the sheet is cleared and a new drawing starts.
- Exact things (plots) are clean, printed-looking cards laid onto the
  drawing, not hand drawn.
- Drawings are still once drawn: no wobbling, no idle motion.

The user's taste in this course: dark, neutral-to-navy ground; nothing
startling; pauses only as long as something is happening.

## The look here

- **Ground:** dark WWT navy, lightest behind the drawing, deeper toward the
  corners, with a fine still grain like a sheet. The title sits on the same
  ground with two soft pools of blue and indigo light drifting slowly; the
  light fades away as the title leaves.
- **No hand on screen.** Lines draw themselves, stroke by stroke, in the
  order a person would draw them (letters left to right; a doodle outline
  first, then its details).
- **One marker.** Round-tipped felt marker, slightly uneven weight; the
  lines wander a little, like a hand, not a plotter. Line weight about 4-6
  px at 1920x1080; bold marks (a cross, a key divider) up to 8 px.
- **Lettering:** one casual hand-lettered print (mixed case, like neat
  whiteboard writing). Every word the viewer reads is lettered, except
  commands, which appear as a clean monospace line on a printed card (this
  chapter has none). Hierarchy comes from size and color only. Sizes are
  cap heights: headline 60-70 px, main content 44-70 px, labels 28-34 px,
  nothing under 26 px.
- **How things appear:** written or drawn on, at a hand's pace: a word
  takes about as long as it takes to say; a small doodle 0.5-1.5 s. A
  heavy element can also "slide in like a card" if it's a printed card.
- **How things change:** the user asked whether moved pieces should slide
  or be erased and redrawn. Decide per beat and say why. The options:
  slide (it keeps identity: the eye tracks one thing); re-color in a
  left-to-right sweep, as if gone over with a second marker; circle,
  underline, or box it; cross it out or scribble over it; wipe the sheet
  clean (a quick fade or slide-off of the whole drawing) at a scene
  change. Don't move drawn things around a lot: the medium is drawing.

## Color roles (WWT palette; all measured on the navy ground)

| Role | Color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| main marker | near-white (gray-50) | most lines and lettering |
| secondary | pale lavender-gray (navy-25) | labels, notes, "not yet named" |
| ghost | mid gray (navy-50) | superseded or background content |
| accent A | light blue (blue-50) | one side of a comparison (was cyan "network") |
| accent B | light orange (orange-50) | the other side (was green "host") |
| key | gold | the one thing the reveal is about (was amber) |
| problem | light red (red-50) | always with a drawn cross, never color alone |
| success | green | check marks, thick strokes only |

Red-50 and orange-50 are close in hue: never let red carry meaning in a
frame where orange does without its cross. Only the main marker and
secondary colors sit on the title light.

## Doodles

Please use doodles where they explain, not decorate: the reference's
doodles carry the ideas (a machine wondering who its neighbors are; a home,
an office, a lab living inside a private range; a message trying to reach
the internet and getting stopped). Keep them minimal line art in the
course's colors. Any object is fine to draw (computers, laptops, a rack,
a router box, buildings, clouds, envelopes, a fence, a ruler, stick
figures); no real brands or logos, no photographs.

## Fixed for this test

- Narration is the recorded take; no re-recording (Narration changes
  should stay empty unless something is impossible).
- Holds can only sit at sentence breaks. Five are already measured and
  can be used at any length: after "32 ones and zeros.", after "on that
  network.", after "The 42 is the host.", after "the split moves left.",
  after "only half the story." A hold at any other sentence break is
  possible; name it and say why, and the caller will measure it. The lead
  -in before the first word is currently 1.0 s.
- The chapter's sound is already approved: a very low, flat lo-fi music
  bed starting as the title leaves, a soft swell under the title, and one
  soft effect when the divider comes to rest after the reveal. Keep the
  Sound section to changes you want (for example, if the reveal moves).
- Chapter 1 of a multi-chapter video: title card first, no thank-you
  card, the tail ends on clean ground for the editor's transition.
